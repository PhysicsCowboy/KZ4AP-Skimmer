# Sources of real on-air CW text for the text model

Research date: 2026-10-14. Web research only (WebSearch, WebFetch); no datasets downloaded. A few
small text samples, web pages and PDFs were fetched to a temporary folder outside the repository to
read them; none is committed.

**Purpose.** The decoder's text model needs probabilities of letter and word sequences in real CW
traffic, built and tested on text that is independent of our own synthetic generator
(`kz4ap_synth.messages`). Two kinds of traffic matter: conversations ("ragchew" QSOs) and skimmer
traffic (CQ calls, callsigns, contest and DX exchanges).

**Labels.** Every claim is labeled **[fact]** (stated by the source at the URL given; quotes are
the source's own words) or **[inference]** (my reasoning from the cited facts). Where I could read
only a search-engine summary and not the page itself, the claim is labeled **[fact, search
summary]**. Pages were read through the WebFetch tool, which returns a summary of the page
written by a small language model; quotes it returned are reproduced as returned and were not
checked against the raw page, except where a label says "raw" (raw file, HTML or listing read),
"text extracted" or "measured here": then I read the raw HTML, text or PDF myself.
**[fact, measured here]** marks a number I measured myself, with the method given. Counts that
WebFetch returned were wrong in four of the cases I checked against the raw files (the W1AW
20 WPM archive: "approximately 180" files, actually 288; Morse Code Ninja phrase file: "2,667
lines", actually 624; ITU-R M.1172: "roughly 200+" abbreviations, about 60; CQ WW public LCRs
2024 CW: 10, actually 11), while counts the source states in its own words checked out (CQ WW
and WPX "Number of logs found", the CQ WW CW 2013 log-checking figures). So any count not marked
"measured here" or quoted from the source's own text should be read as approximate. Terms of use
are quoted or linked as the source states them; I do not interpret them legally. Sizes are as the
source states them unless labeled [inference] (my estimate, with the arithmetic).

**Kinds.** *Real*: text actually sent on the air by amateur operators (copied by a human, decoded
by a machine, or typed from a log). *Practice*: material written for training, modeled on QSOs,
not real traffic. *Synthetic*: generated by a program from templates or statistics.

**Out of scope** (owner): the Reverse Beacon Network, and callsign databases.

**Layout.** A ranked summary table; then one numbered section per source, in rank order;
then supporting notes and negative results (what was searched and not found); then Gaps.

## Sources, in rank order

### 1. CQ WW DX Contest public logs (Cabrillo)

- **URL:** https://cqww.com/publiclogs/ ; per year and mode, e.g.
  https://cqww.com/publiclogs/2024cw/ ; one log per call, e.g.
  https://cqww.com/publiclogs/2024cw/k0acp.log
- **Contains:** the submitted logs of all entrants except checklogs, 2005 to 2025, CW and SSB
  [fact, https://cqww.com/publiclogs/]: "It is our policy to make the logs of all entries (except
  checklogs) public"; address and e-mail fields are removed [fact, same page]. 2024 CW: "Number of
  logs found: 7961" [fact, https://cqww.com/publiclogs/2024cw/].
- **Real or not:** real on-air contacts, entered by the operators in logging software during the
  contest [fact for the format; inference for the process]. Each QSO line holds frequency (kHz),
  mode, date, UTC time, own call, RST sent, zone sent, other call, RST received, zone received,
  e.g. `QSO: 14043 CW 2024-11-23 0034 K0ACP 599 4 ED1R 599 14` [fact,
  https://cqww.com/publiclogs/2024cw/k0acp.log]. The exchange is "RST report plus CQ Zone of the
  station transmitter location (e.g., 599 05)" [fact, https://cqww.com/rules/].
- **What it gives the text model:** real callsign *frequencies in traffic* (who is actually on the
  air, how often, on which band), real zone and RST distributions, real timing between contacts,
  and the run/search-and-pounce structure (from times and frequencies). It does **not** give the
  transmitted text itself: what was sent ("5NN" vs "599", "TU", "UP", "AGN", repeats) is not
  logged, and RST is logged as 599 whatever was sent [inference].
- **Size:** for CQ WW CW 2024, "we received 8313 logs this time around representing a staggering
  total of 5,409,004 Q[SOs]" [fact, https://cqww.com/results/2024_cq_ww_dx_cw_results.pdf, text
  extracted with a scratch tool]; 7 961 of those logs are public [fact, directory page]. So about
  5 million logged QSO lines per year in CW alone, each contact usually logged by both stations
  [inference].
- **Format/access:** Cabrillo 3.0 text, one file per call, plain HTTP; no bulk archive offered
  [fact, directory page]. Scraping is the only bulk route [inference].
- **Terms (as stated):** rules: by submitting, entrants agree that "the log entry may be made open
  to the public" [fact, https://cqww.com/rules/]; page footer "Copyright (c) 2008-2026 World Wide
  Radio Operators Foundation, Inc. All Rights Reserved." [fact, https://cqww.com/publiclogs/].
- **Quality:** the logs are what the operators *copied*, including their busts (wrong calls,
  wrong zones); the WPX page says its published logs are "as used" in log checking, i.e. not
  corrected [fact for WPX wording; inference that CQ WW is the same]. Some header fields carry
  operator names (`NAME:`) [fact, sample log].

  Reserved" [fact, https://contests.arrl.org/publiclogs.php]; no separate terms for the logs on
  that page [fact].
- **Exchanges:** see "Other contest logs, and the exchanges the logs encode" below.

### 4. IZ4KBW archive of real CW QSO recordings (audio only, no text)

- **URL:** https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1XaF3Mvwa6WvgOCzZjp0NwzgnYdNHsW2Q (Google Drive),
  announced in "Large archive of CW off-air recordings from Italy", 2021-11-30,
  https://swling.com/blog/2021/11/large-archive-of-cw-off-air-recordings-from-italy/
- **Contains:** recordings of real on-air ragchew QSOs by Luigi Ciampoli, IZ4KBW: "Some time ago
  with a friend of mine I created a very big archive of CW QSO recordings available for everybody
  who want to listen to CW traffic. The idea was born by the need to give to newcomer HAMS
  material to train on real CW traffic on the bands..." [fact, swling.com post]; organized by
  "date/calls of participants/frequencies/keys used and WPM speed"; "thousands of records", some
  with "ex professional radio operators discussing the past golden era" [fact, same post, via
  WebFetch summary]. The Drive root holds year folders 2017 to 2026
  and folders "AA_telegrams", "TFC FROM COASTAL STATIONS" (maritime coast-station recordings),
  "Uncertified Transmission recordings" and "Unspecified date" [fact, raw Drive listing HTML read
  2026-10-14]. The 2025 folder has about 50 dated subfolders, each holding one MP3 named like
  `20250201_<call> <call>_paddle_30 wpm_10117 khz.mp3` [fact, raw Drive listing HTML].
- **Real or not:** real on-air QSOs, mostly Italian stations on 80 m and 30 m (frequencies such as
  3557 kHz, 10117 kHz in the file names) [fact for the sampled file names; inference for
  "mostly"].
- **Text:** **none**. No transcript or text file appears in the folders I listed (root, 2025, three
  dated subfolders, "AA_telegrams", "TFC FROM COASTAL STATIONS"); the telegram folder pairs MP3/AMR
  audio with JPG images (photos of telegram forms, presumably) [fact, raw Drive listing HTML; "presumably"
  is inference]. Text would have to be produced by human copy or machine decoding.
- **Size:** about 50 recordings in 2025 alone; total "thousands of records" (the archive owner's
  claim) [fact, as cited above]. Hours not stated.
- **Language/dates:** CW in English-style ham abbreviations by Italian and other European
  operators [inference from callsigns]; 2017 to 2026 [fact].
- **Terms (as stated):** "available for everybody who want to listen to CW traffic"; "We
  released this large archive of recordings in a Google drive folder that can be shared." [fact,
  swling.com post]; no license on the Drive folder [fact].
- **Value:** the largest public collection of real *ragchew* CW audio I found; a possible
  human-transcription target for a real-text test set [inference].

### 5. CQ WW / CQ WPX log-checking statistics and public log checking reports (real copy errors)

- **Aggregate statistics, CQ WW CW 2013:** "4,871,227 (88.5%) QSO checked against another log";
  "73,087 ( 1.5%) busted calls"; "46,467 ( 1.0%) not in log"; "22,165 ( 0.5%) busted exchange";
  "39,950 ( 0.7%) QSO with unique calls"; "22,495 (56.3%) unique calls busted"; "36.5% of the
  total entrants had an error rate of 2.0% or less. The median error rate was 2.7%."; "7,442 logs
  total" [fact, raw HTML of https://cqww.com/blog/cq-ww-cw-2013-log-checking-reports/]. CQ WW CW
  2014: 1.5% busted calls and 0.8% not-in-log of 5 848 165 QSOs [fact, search summary of
  https://cqww.com/blog/cq-ww-cw-2014-log-checking-statistics/].
- **Meaning for us:** about 1.5% of the callsigns in the public CW logs are copying errors that log
  checking found; the published logs are not corrected [inference from the two facts above and the
  "as used" wording on the WPX public-log page]. Any callsign or exchange statistics taken from the
  logs carry this error floor.
- **Public LCRs (top entries only):** https://cqww.com/publiclcr/ (CQ WW, CW and SSB, 2012 to
  2024; e.g. https://cqww.com/publiclcr/2024cw/ lists 11 reports: 4o4a, cn3a, cr3a, d4dx, k1ar, p3x,
  tk0c, un4q, v26k, vp5m, zd7vj `.rpt` [fact, measured here on the raw HTML]): "To help you
  understand the value of the reports we are providing the log check reports of world high scoring
  entries." [fact, page]. CQ WPX: https://cqwpx.com/publiclcr/ (2010-2013 and 2020-2022) "To provide
  a standard for comparison we are making public the Log Checking Reports of the World high scoring
  stations in various categories." [fact, page]. All other LCRs go privately by e-mail [fact,
  https://cqww.com/publiclcr/].
- **What a report holds:** sections "Not In Log", "Incorrect Call", "Incorrect Exchange
  Information", "Unique Calls", "Stations Copying <CALL> Incorrectly", "Stations Copying <CALL>
  Exchange Incorrectly" and others; incorrect-call lines give the logged and the correct call, e.g.
  `7053 CW 2024-11-23 0032 D4DX 35 K3UA 05 correct K3AU`, `7054 ... SP5X 15 correct DP5X`; summary
  for D4DX 2024 CW: "9707 Raw QSO before checking; 9563 Final QSO after checking reductions; 1.5%
  error rate; ... 40 not in log; 80 incorrect calls; 24 incorrect exchanges" [fact,
  https://cqww.com/publiclcr/2024cw/d4dx.rpt via WebFetch].
- **Real or not:** real human copy errors in on-air CW, with the "truth" established by
  cross-checking against the other station's log [fact for the method as described; the truth is
  itself a log-checking judgment, inference].
- **Size:** 11 reports for CQ WW 2024 CW [fact, measured here], about 10 per contest, mode and
  year [inference from that one year]; on the order of 100 incorrect calls per report [inference
  from the D4DX example: 80], so roughly 10^4 real call-confusion pairs over the CW years available
  [inference: about 20 contest-years (CQ WW 13, WPX 7) x 10 reports x 50 to 100, plus the "copying
  incorrectly" sections].
- **Terms:** no terms beyond the WWROF copyright footer of the sites [fact, as for the public logs].
- **Value:** the only open source of *real copy-error pairs* in CW traffic found (K3UA for K3AU:
  transposition; SP5X for DP5X: S/D confusion); relevant to the text model's handling of
  near-miss callsigns and to any error model [inference].
- **Excluded:** N6TV's "How fast do people send in WW CW?" (about 30 WPM) is computed from Reverse
  Beacon Network data [fact, search summary of
  https://cqww.com/blog/how-fast-do-people-send-in-ww-cw/], so it is out of scope.
- **Individual example:** CT1BOH's own CQ WW CW 2003 entry: 1.0% bad calls (76 of 7 938 QSOs),
  0.4% not-in-log (34), 59.4% of contacts cross-checked [fact, https://www.qsl.net/ct1boh/accuracy.htm
  via WebFetch].

### 7. IW5EDI "200 Morse Code QSOs" (practice text)

- **URL:** http://www.iw5edi.com/ham-radio/2097/2097; files
  http://www.iw5edi.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/MORSE1.txt and `.../MORSE2.txt`.
- **Contains:** 200 single overs of ragchew QSOs "which might typically be heard on the HF bands",
  "designed to give people who are learning Morse Code some practice with real life Morse
  contacts, rather than using text passages from books, or random letters generated by computer"
  [fact, page]; meant to be played by the Supermorse training program [fact, page via WebFetch
  summary].
- **Real or not:** practice material, composed by a person (not template-generated: each over is
  different) with invented callsigns, names and QTHs [inference from the sample]. Original author
  not named on the page [fact].
- **Sample (verbatim, first over of MORSE1.txt):** `2E4ART DE DJ5YXZ / GD OM TKS FER RPRT FROM HULL
  = UR RST 459 WID SOME QSB = HR NAME HANS QTH BONN = ANT MAG LOOP RIG IC729 = WX IS COLD = PSE QSL
  VIA BURO AR / 2E4ART DE DJ5YTR VA` [fact, raw file read]. Note the inconsistent sign-off call
  (DJ5YXZ vs DJ5YTR) in the original.
- **Size:** 19 309 + 19 770 bytes, 4 318 + 4 434 words [fact, measured here: `wc -c` and `wc -w`
  on the two files as downloaded].
- **Format/access:** two plain-text files, uppercase, `=` for BT, `AR`, `KN`, `VA`/`SK` as letter
  pairs [fact, sample].
- **Language/dates:** English; uploaded 2016-03 (URL path) [fact]; content date unknown.
- **Terms (as stated):** none on the page [fact].
- **Value:** a compact, independent, human-written ragchew-over corpus with realistic structure
  (RST, name, QTH, rig, antenna, WX, QSL); good for a *test* of the text model on ragchew style;
  too small to build one [inference].

## Gaps

1. **No transcripts of real ragchew QSOs in any useful quantity** (priority 1 of the brief is
   essentially empty). Searches of the open web, Hugging Face, Kaggle, Zenodo, GitHub and arXiv on
   2026-10-14 found only single transcripts (cwlab's one QSO, e04's three short clips) and one
   unpublished set (morseformer, 31 min) [fact, sections 17 and 21 and "Negative results"]. Real
   ragchew *audio* exists (IZ4KBW, thousands of recordings; cwlab; HamSCI's whole-band recordings),
   so a real-text test set would have to be made by transcribing it: by ear, or by machine decoding
   with human correction [inference]. The IZ4KBW archive carries no license, only "can be shared";
   the HamSCI recordings are CC BY 4.0 [fact, sections 4 and 10].
2. **No word or n-gram statistics of real CW conversation text.** Nothing found gives word,
   bigram or phrase frequencies measured on real QSOs. Lists of "common" or "top 100" words and
   abbreviations do not say how they were derived [fact, section 14]. Pierpont's letter counts are
   English text, not ham traffic [fact, https://www.qsl.net/ng3p/haminfo/pierpont/c28.htm: "taken
   from one of the common books on cryptanalysis, based on number of occurrences per thousand of
   normal English text material"]. Bell (1977) used Shannon's English figures (see
   `../../bell-1977-notes.md`). The only real-traffic statistics found, VE3NEA's character and
   word-length table from CW Skimmer decodes, are already used by our generator and decoder, so they
   cannot test the text model independently; their corpus is unpublished and machine-decoded
   [fact, section 9].
3. **Logs are fields, not the transmitted text.** The public logs give callsigns, exchanges,
   bands and times, but not what was keyed: "5NN" versus "599", cut numbers ("5NN TT" for 599 00),
   "TU", "UP", "AGN", "?", repeats, partial calls, "CQ TEST" versus "TEST", and every RST is
   logged as 599 whatever was sent [inference]. Real sent text for contest traffic would need
   audio (K5ZD, HamSCI) or a reconstruction from the operators' sending macros plus the log; no
   collection of real contest macros was looked for [inference].
4. **No non-RBN source for CQ-call forms and their frequencies** (CQ, CQ TEST, CQ POTA, CQ SOTA,
   CQ DX, CQ CWT, CQ SST, CQ SKCC...). The RBN, which would have them, is out of scope (owner).
   [fact: none found; the RBN exclusion is the owner's].
5. **Errors in the logs.** About 1.5% of logged CW calls are busts that log checking finds (CQ WW
   CW 2013 and 2014), and the published logs are not corrected; corrected pairs are public only
   for about 10 top entries per contest, mode and year [fact, section 5].
6. **Names, QTHs, rigs and weather in ragchews:** no real distribution found; all practice texts
   invent them [fact for the texts read]. Real first names as sent on the air exist in contest
   call-history files (CWT, SST, NAQP exchanges), but those are callsign databases, out of scope
   here (owner) [inference for their content; not checked].
7. **Licensing.** Explicitly open: HamSCI logs and recordings (CC BY 4.0), the IARU EOP and the
   Wikipedia lists (CC BY-SA 4.0), Morse Code Ninja (GPL-3.0 repository), Patater qso-generator
   (Apache-2.0 repository), VE3NEA DeepCW (MIT). Public by policy but all rights reserved: CQ WW,
   CQ WPX, CQ 160 (WWROF) and ARRL logs. Restrictive: ARRL web pages ("Reproduction ... without
   written permission is strictly prohibited") and CW Academy. No terms at all: IZ4KBW, IW5EDI,
   K5ZD [fact, as quoted in the sections]. These are the sources' own statements; I do not
   interpret them.
8. **Not checked or not found:** public logs for NAQP and the NA Sprint (only results PDFs found),
   WAE (QTC traffic), RDXC, RSGB contests and state QSO parties; SOTA and POTA activation logs; the
   contents of the K7QO course; ACP 131 (cited by Wikipedia for prosigns); the HamSCI SEQP CW share
   [fact: not found or not read on 2026-10-14].
9. **Other registers, not CW:** FT8 and other digital-mode message logs (PSKReporter), SSB voice
   sets on Zenodo, and maritime coast-station CW traffic (IZ4KBW's "TFC FROM COASTAL STATIONS"
   folder) exist but are not amateur CW conversation [fact for their existence as cited;
   relevance is inference].
10. **Out of scope by the owner's decision:** the Reverse Beacon Network (including statistics
    derived from it, such as N6TV's CQ WW CW sending speeds) and callsign databases (SCP, call
    history).
